A guide for everyone

How to prepare for climate hazards in Sri Lanka

Simple steps for your home, your community, and our shared future.

Start with your family plan ↓

Why being prepared matters

Precautionary measures and risk reduction are always safer and more cost-effective than reactionary scrambling after an emergency strikes.

This guide walks you through everything you need to know to handle climate hazards and stay safe. Take a look and share it with someone who needs it.

Disclaimer: This is not an extensive list, but a starting point to prompt us to think, equip ourselves, stay aware, and prepare better through strategic planning that aims to protect people and the natural environments we need to survive.

01 · Home

Your family plan.

1

Know your local risks

  • Understand which climate hazards, such as flash floods, landslides, or dry spells, your specific region faces most.
  • Speak with community leaders, traditional practitioners, and the elderly to understand what has changed over the years, for better or worse (even unique perspectives matter), and identify the resources and knowledge available in your local area.
2

Home management systems

  • Implement micro-infrastructure around home systems to manage excess water, whether that’s heavy rain and flooding or holding onto moisture for dry periods and drought.
  • Create a “natural sponge effect” around your property by building rain gardens, swales, and planting pockets of native vegetation that absorb runoff and stabilize soil.
3

The family emergency go-bag

  • Pack an emergency go-bag containing essential supplies, first aid, non-perishable food, and clean water.
  • Keep both digital copies and handwritten physical lists of emergency contacts, medical details, vital information and key documents inside this bag, and keep it in a safe area.
4

Household communication plan

  • Discuss and plan clear family protocols: who to contact, designated safe meeting points, and evacuation routes.
  • Prioritize the safety of vulnerable members, including children, the elderly, and individuals with medical needs. Every member, including children, MUST know precisely what to do.
5

Infrastructure familiarization

  • Know the exact routes and locations of the nearest hospitals, schools, and designated safe evacuation shelters.
  • Get in touch with your Local Government Authorities (Grama Sevaka, Niladharis, Padesha Sabha, etc.) and request their support in identifying and organising community drills and awareness sessions.
02 · Neighbourhood

Stronger communities, together.

Keep emergency hotlines handy

Identify local council offices, police stations, and key authorities (such as the Disaster Management Centre Local Authority, or other helplines). Keep both printed and digital copies of their hotlines.

Check on vulnerable neighbours

Keep in touch with remote, less-equipped, or high-risk populations in your area who may need extra help during a climate shock.

Know safe routes

Plan clear community transit routes so everyone knows how to safely navigate away from hazard zones.

Rely on verified updates

Only stay informed through verified sources like official government updates and the weather department. Avoid unverified rumours.

Run emergency drills

Involve local schools and community groups in safety programs so citizens know how to act during a climate emergency.

Vote & invest for long-term protection

Support local policies and leaders that prioritize environmental preservation and community safety over short-term commercial profits.

Establish an Independent Monitoring & Observations Committee

To provide insights, reports, concerns, and updates on changes and possible trajectories in your local area. This promotes active agency, community-led monitoring and management within the region. The committee should involve youth and data analysts who can contribute to and build the community’s capacity while ensuring the work matches on-ground realities.

Conduct risk & vulnerability mapping

Identify the risks that threaten and impact your local area or community, while assessing vulnerable groups, individuals and areas to provide immediate relief and support in case of emergencies.

03 · The bigger picture

Building long-term resilience.

Nature

Stabilize ecosystems

Invest in green infrastructure by planting trees and creating pocket forests to help stabilize surrounding land and absorb heavy rainfall.

Nature

Biodiversity as a solution

Climate change is a cross-cutting impact that affects all sectors (a multi-sectoral, multi-layered issue). However, biodiversity is a solution we can bring forward that protects and can enhance all sectors and livelihoods.

Place

Unique eco-climatic composition

Sri Lanka’s unique eco-climatic composition means climate impacts are felt differently, so they need to be dealt with uniquely, in alignment with local knowledge, information, practices, and people.

Nature

Protect natural buffers

Safeguard existing forests, wetlands, and coastal environments that naturally shield communities from extreme weather.

Principles

Use rights-based, values-based systems

Promote sustainable, transformative climate measures that stay updated with global data and community needs.

Principles

Ensure equity, fairness & inclusivity

Protect the wellbeing and resources of both current communities and future generations.
